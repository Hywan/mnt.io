    Speedrun a client at the Matrix Conference

    November 05, 2026 4 minutes read Keywords: matrix, messaging, client, rust Edit this page

    Malmö. Thursday, October 21, 2026. 3:15pm. I was back on stage at the Matrix Conference, live and broadcasted on Internet, to do a speedrun of a Matrix client with the Matrix Rust SDK in 20 minutes.

    Matrix is an open network for secure, decentralised communication. It is an important technology for Internet.

    Matrix is a protocol. Everyone can implement it: either by providing its own server and connect it to the federation, or by providing its own client and connect it to the federation too. Nobody has a full control over the network, and nobody controls the clients nor the servers. And yet, end-to-end encryption is working, synchronisation is working, and everybody can talk to everybody, communities organise themselves, the network grows and grows.

    I am working at Element since 4 years now. I am sponsored to work on the Matrix Rust SDK, a project owned by the Matrix organisation. Everything we do is available to the entire Matrix community, not only for Element. Well, this is the open source world.

    In this context, we thought it would have been nice to demonstrate how to build a Matrix client in 20 minutes; just for fun. What a silly idea, sight! It is obviously a difficult exercise. Yes, the SDK is getting mature, robust and very easy to use, but, come on, 20 minutes is really short. Anyway, I've enjoyed doing it, and the feedback were great! Thanks to anyone who has watched it!

    Other Talks#

    All the talks are available online, including talks from the public sector, such as Europe, Brazil, Dutch etc. Let's pick some technical talks from my good friends.

    What's New in the Matrix Rust SDK#

    The creator and tech lead of the project, Damir Jelić, presents what's new and what's changed this year in the Matrix Rust SDK. And yes, we have merged 1089 pull requests from 73 unique authors in a year, that's crazy for such a small team.

    How Encryption Works in Matrix#

    Andy Balaam has this particular, rare talent to make a complex topic simple to understand. If you want to learn about how encryption works in Matrix, this is a must watch. I mean, really.

    New Phone Who Dis: User Verification Using X.509 Certificates#

    Richard van der Hoff has the same superpower than Andy: concisely explain non-trivial concepts. If you don't know what X.509 certificates are, or if you are curious to see how we can use them in Matrix to automatically ensure you're chatting with the right person, not an intruder, without reading out numbers of emojis, this talk is for you.

    About Transport#

    I currently live in Switzerland. The conference was in Sweden. Europe has a fantastic rail network, and more importantly, a unique night train network!

    The trip by train has generated 9.17kg CO2eq, be 0.45% of the annual carbon budget (for a sustainable world, we should all be at 2000kg CO2eq maximum). Going there by plane would have generated 848kg CO2eq, be 42% of the annual carbon budget. It's 92 times more than the train.

    Not only train is way less polluting, it's also cheaper! The full trip, 2900km total, including night train, has costed 406EUR. With a flight, it would have been around 520EUR, if I include trains to reach the airport, we are about 600EUR.

    If you are curious to try night train, you can check this map that lists all possible connections, stops, companies operating the trains etc. Taking the night train is a nice way of travelling, and it saves a lot of carbon emissions.

    I've taken regular day trains to go to Malmö, and a night train to come back home from Hamburg.
